01

Research Question

Do people living in Almaty and the other regions most affected by the January 2022 unrest show a larger decline in institutional trust in government — from before January 2022 to now — than people living in less-affected regions?
Hardest-hit (comparison group) Less-affected Schematic, not to scale
02

Why This Is Worth Asking

  • The January events may not have affected every region the same way
  • If trust fell more in some regions, the population could become divided
  • Some regions might keep supporting the government while others turn away
  • This shows whether Qantar left a lasting regional divide
03

Epistemological Positioning

Positivist.

  • Uses survey data to measure trust as percentages
  • Compares those percentages between groups of regions
  • Compares trust before January 2022 with trust now
04

Contribution

What does this study add to existing research?

What existing research shows

  • Trust in government is low, and it is measured for the country as a whole (Sadykova; OECD)
  • Large surveys cover every region but only publish national totals (KazISS; Bureau of National Statistics)
  • The unrest hit some regions harder than others (BTI)

What this study adds

  • Trust broken down by region, before and after January 2022
  • A test of whether the January events affected trust differently from region to region
  • A useful answer either way: a bigger drop in hard-hit regions supports the mechanism; an equal drop points to a nationwide cause

Literature Review

I
Trust in government in Kazakhstan
Low trust is a recognised problem, but it is only measured for the country as a whole.
Sadykova, K. (2024). "Public Trust in Government in Kazakhstan's Framework of Good Governance." Мемлекеттік басқару және мемлекеттік қызмет, №2 (89).
  • Looks at low trust through a "good governance" lens
  • Trust in law enforcement rose from 2022 to 2023 but stayed low
  • In 2022 only 52.5% felt able to influence government actions
  • No regional breakdown
OECD (2025). OECD Public Governance Scan of Kazakhstan. OECD Publishing.
  • Confirms an official government-trust survey has run since 2022
  • Compares Kazakhstan with 30 OECD countries
  • No regional breakdown
II
Where the trust numbers come from
Large surveys cover every region, but only publish national totals.
Kazakhstan Institute for Strategic Studies (KazISS / KISI) — national trust surveys, kisi.kz, 2026.
  • Recurring survey of ~8,000 people across all 17 regions and 3 cities
  • 76.4% trust in the President in its 2025 survey
  • Publishes national totals only, so it is the best candidate for a data request
Bureau of National Statistics, Agency for Strategic Planning and Reforms. "The level of public confidence in law enforcement agencies and the judiciary." Oct–Nov 2023.
  • 18,005 households in all regions
  • Crime data is published by region (Almaty highest), but trust only nationally
III
The January events
Why the unrest, and the crackdown, hit some regions harder than others.
Bertelsmann Stiftung (2026). BTI 2026 Country Report — Kazakhstan.
  • Independent governance index; dates the unrest to 2–11 January 2022
  • Links it to the "socially unstable western regions", the basis for my region list
  • 10,000+ people detained in January 2022 alone
  • No trust data: supports the mechanism and region choice
The gap Trust is measured, and the unrest was regional, but no one reports trust by region. That is what this study adds.
06

Theory and Hypotheses

  • Independent variable: Region of residence, as a proxy for exposure
    • Hard-hit: Almaty city, Mangystau, Turkestan, Akmola
    • Chosen using BTI (2026)
  • Dependent variable: Institutional trust in government (broadly)
  • Hypothesis: Hard-hit regions show a larger drop in trust from before January 2022 to now
  • Causal mechanism:
    • People nearer the unrest saw or felt the crackdown (arrests, force, 238 deaths)
    • Seeing the state use force against civilians lowers trust in the state
Limitation
  • Almaty could show a bigger drop in trust for a reason unrelated to the crackdown
  • It is more urbanised than most of the country
  • People there see and consume much more news and information
  • That alone could lower trust
